03 — how the portfolio actually works here

The tooling forces the discipline — it isn't just tracked, it's rationed

Tracking capacity is finite by design. That's not a limitation to work around — it's the thing that keeps this a portfolio of convictions instead of a list of everything that looked interesting.

add

A position enters, a tracking credit spends

Every project added to the portfolio costs a real, finite tracking credit. There's no unlimited tier — the ceiling itself is what forces an actual allocation decision instead of holding everything at once.

rescan

The thesis gets re-checked, not just the price

Rescan re-runs the evaluation against current conditions. This is the actual mechanism for narrative-backed holding — not a feeling that it's still good, a re-verification that the reasons it was held in the first place still hold.

outcome

Closing the loop honestly is what makes the accuracy record real

Every position eventually gets a logged outcome. Decisive ones — success or rugged — are what the accuracy record above is built from. A position left open indefinitely with no outcome logged doesn't strengthen the record, it just sits outside it.

04 — the holding rule

Default is hold. Exit needs a reason, not a mood.

Hold, by default

Listing day isn't an exit signal. The position stays open until one of the two conditions on the right actually triggers — not because it moved, not because it's been a while.

Exit triggers on two conditions only

  • Thesis breaks — a Rescan shows the reason it was held no longer holds. Logged as the outcome, not disguised as anything softer.
  • Target reached — the specific narrative-backed target set at entry actually arrives. Not "it's up," the target itself.
"Held for a reason, exited for a reason — never for a mood."
